Radar is a promising sensing modality for unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)
detect-and-avoid (DAA), offering long-range, all-weather detection
independent of target cooperation. Recent miniaturisation of millimetre-wave
radar hardware and the availability of capable embedded GPU compute have made
onboard radar processing feasible for small UAV platforms. However, existing
work consistently stops short of validating a complete sensing-to-tracking
pipeline under realistic airborne conditions, has not benchmarked GPU-based
radar implementations under UAV size, weight, and power (SWaP) constraints,
and has only evaluated angle estimation algorithms in idealised noise rather
than in end-to-end pipelines. This thesis addresses these gaps by designing,
implementing, and evaluating a software-defined monopulse radar tracking
pipeline on NVIDIA Jetson Xavier embedded GPU platforms, systematically
comparing algorithm choices and low-level implementations across hardware
configurations. The pipeline achieves real-time multi-target tracking with
end-to-end latency below 50\,ms. Taylor windows calibrated to the CFAR noise
floor are found to minimise false alarms without recall penalty; coherent
integration raises detection recall from near zero to 0.9 in the worst noise
regime tested; and CA-CFAR outperforms competing variants in high-noise
conditions. GPU-based CFAR is shown to be the preferred implementation on the
Xavier platform, while the optimal CPU-versus-GPU crossover differs between
embedded and desktop configurations, demonstrating that implementation
selection cannot be made platform-agnostically. Monopulse angle sensing
is validated as an unbiased bearing and elevation estimator even at
$-10$\,dB SNR, and a Kalman-filter-based multi-target tracker maintains
position errors below 22\,m RMS across all noise regimes tested. A live
airborne field trial successfully geolocated static ground targets at up to
706\,m, confirming physical pipeline validity while exposing a hardware noise
artefact that prevented tracking of the aerial target. Together, these results
provide hardware-aware design guidance for engineers developing compact radar
DAA systems and establish an empirical foundation for future work on
SWaP-constrained airborne radar tracking.
